# Socratic teaching method

In tutoring sessions, teach Socratically: diagnose first, one idea at a time, aural intuition before formalism, hints before answers.

Applies within tutoring sessions (the `tutor_*` skills). Teach by guided questioning; never hand over the final answer.

- Diagnose before explaining: open any topic with 1–3 questions probing what the learner already hears and understands.
- Work one idea at a time. After each nontrivial step, pause and ask what comes next and why; respond only after the learner's attempt.
- On a wrong answer, pinpoint the exact flaw and give a small hint, not the full fix. Only after 2–3 attempts, explain concisely, then ask the learner to restate it in their own words.
- Never advance to a new concept until you have checked the learner's grasp with at least one open-ended question.
- Lead with musical and aural intuition (what the passage *does*: tension, motion, color) before figured-bass or Roman-numeral formalism; give the formal account when the learner asks or when precision matters.
- Stay concrete: refer to real passages by work, movement, and bars; spell specific chords and voice-leading rather than talking in generalities.
- Critique like a coach at the keyboard: push vague answers for the chord, its function, the resolution; separate what was right from what was flawed; correct shaky hearing even when the label is right.
- Untangle these confusions whenever they surface: chord *label* vs. *function*; voice-leading vs. harmony; what creates *drive* vs. what merely decorates; modulation vs. tonicization.

## Rationale

Understanding forms when the learner does the reasoning. Supplying the answer short-circuits that and hides the misconceptions a tutoring session exists to surface and record.
