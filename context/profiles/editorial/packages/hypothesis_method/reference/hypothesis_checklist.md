# The hypothesis checklist: five hypotheses before any editorial record

Investigation always precedes the record. When something in the print looks different (an omitted dot, a deviating mark, a missing figure), the hypotheses are formed first, each with its evidence for and against, and only then is anything written into the decision's hypothesis table. The order is binding: a hypothesis written down before its evidence was gathered is already a preference, not an investigation.

## The counter-evidence test, before anything is written

Before a hypothesis is born, run the counter-evidence test: *state which observation would have produced the opposite outcome.* Say the hypothesis is "the bassoon gets no dots because the difference is intentional": what would have been seen if the difference were instead an accidental omission? (For example, the dotted figure breaking off in the middle of a series, or the remains of dots the printer dropped.) If you cannot say what the opposite outcome would look like in the source material, the question is not yet formulated as something distinct from its answer: investigate further before writing. The hypothesis that passes the test then gathers its own counter-evidence with the same seriousness: finding support is not enough; search conscientiously for what speaks against it.

## The family rule: look at every member of the family, not only the seed

The common mistake is to lock onto the seed: the single bar that first caught the eye. The investigation starts with the seed but never judges by it. The pattern lives in the family, in every place that carries the same gesture. Search the whole movement (and across movements) for passages with the same rhythm, contour, intervals, meter and articulation, and save the result as evidence the decision can cite, never writing it over the work's own files. Read every member of the family: does the difference follow the pattern of repetition, or break it? A single deviation without repetition speaks for a mistake; a recurring deviation speaks for intent.

## The five hypotheses: the candidates in the table

Every hypothesis in the decision's table carries its evidence rows for and against. The five:

1. **Omission** — the print lacks something parallel material carries.
   *For:* the family members carry the mark where the seed lacks it; the context calls for the figure. *Against:* no family member carries it; the figure breaks the bar count in a way the composer would have noticed.
2. **Intentional difference** — the difference is the composer's, or the print's own, intent.
   *For:* recurring and pattern-bearing through the family; follows a functional boundary (phrase, gesture, register). *Against:* breaks the pattern where the function does not differ; missing only where the typesetting was under strain.
3. **Misread** — it is the reading, not the print.
   *For:* the print is ambiguous at high resolution; an earlier event in the bar governs the reading (an accidental carried through the bar); the context (clef, courtesy accidental, key) explains it. *Against:* the print is unambiguous on closer inspection; all voices and octaves show the same.
4. **Later correction** — a later source corrects an earlier one.
   *For:* the later edition changes consistently and leaves the rest untouched; the correction follows a known error tradition. *Against:* the later source changes just as arbitrarily; the change has no support in any essential source (the basis is owned by the work's declared basis source, never by a parallel source).
5. **Indeterminate** — the residual case: the evidence does not suffice.
   *For:* the witness is sparse on both sides and the parallels are silent. *Against:* (rows that may later become decisive, for example a newly found family member). The hypothesis lives on until the evidence lands, and the decision's disposition becomes `unresolved`.

## From table to decision

When the table stands, move on to the decision record: the disposition is chosen (the casebook's patterns help, `casebook.md`), the saved evidence is cited by path in the decision, and the marks are tied to the decision. The record stands `proposed`; the skeptical reader goes over the packet blind, and the approval is the human editor's alone. A hypothesis is never a verdict: the edition changes only what the human has approved.
