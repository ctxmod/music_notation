# The casebook: patterns to recognise, never rules to follow

The casebook supports the investigation of an apparent omission. Each case is a **pattern to recognise**: how it looks in the print, which outcome the evidence of the original case supported, what the opposite outcome is and when it applies, and which hypothesis and which disposition the case tests. Reading always follows the hypothesis checklist (`hypothesis_checklist.md`): counter-evidence test and family rule first. Case names are stable; skills and records cite them. The cases come from the edition of an orchestral work whose editorial table was worked through case by case.

**No case is a norm.** A case that cannot be written without becoming a lazy rule ("always supply from the parallel part") does not belong here: it is dropped from the casebook and escalated to the owner of the editorial policy, because a casebook of norms is a catalogue of prejudices. Every case below carries its opposite in the text; if the opposite cannot be formulated, the case is unfinished.

Dispositions used: `supply-from-parallel`, `preserve-source`, `equivalent-reading`, `normalise-notation`, `coordinate-release`, `unresolved`.

## The cases

### shared-gesture-parallel-part
A part lacks accents and slurs that a parallel part carries, within a gesture they share. *Outcome:* the evidence supported **Omission**: the part received the marks from the parallel, disposition `supply-from-parallel`. *The opposite:* when the parallel's marks are its own expressive layer (a soloistic accentuation the other parts deliberately do not carry) and the family shows the same unevenness everywhere, the difference is **Intentional difference** and the disposition `preserve-source`. *The test:* the family members: do they carry the gesture marked where the seed lacks it?

### bounded-dots-within-figure
Dots are supplied only within the shared figure. *Outcome:* **Omission**, but strictly bounded: the supply stops at the figure's boundary, disposition `supply-from-parallel`, with the bound carried in the rationale. *The opposite:* when the dotted figure continues into dissimilar material on the other side of the boundary, supplying there is the editor's invention: `preserve-source` applies (or `unresolved` if the boundary is unclear). *The test:* where the figure's boundaries actually lie, not where the eye wants to draw them.

### recurring-difference-preserved
A part (in the original case, the bassoon) is left without dots, again and again through the whole passage. *Outcome:* **Intentional difference**: the difference is recurring and pattern-bearing; disposition `preserve-source`. A second edition judged a similar echo-gesture the same way: the difference through the whole family carried the intent, and the saved family evidence was cited in the record. *The opposite:* when the dotting breaks off in the middle of a series, or the remains of dropped dots are visible in the print, it is **Omission** (a printing error) and the disposition `supply-from-parallel`. *The test:* does the deviation break the pattern, or is it the pattern?

### written-out-repeats-equivalent
Abbreviated repeats are written out. *Outcome:* the rewriting was made under a **declared reading**: the written-out form is claimed equivalent to the print's abbreviation, disposition `equivalent-reading`. *The opposite:* when the written-out form changes the sounding attack (articulation, attack, length per note), it is not equivalent: `preserve-source`, and the abbreviation stays. *The test:* do the two notations sound the same? Compare the sounding result, not the notation.

### tremolo-two-halves-declared-reading
A tremolo is notated as two halves. *Outcome:* **Intentional difference** in the source's notation, and the edition reproduces it as two halves under a declared reading ("read as tremolo"), disposition `equivalent-reading`. *The opposite:* when the halves are genuinely different textures (different interval or octave, different kind of attack), it is not a notation question at all: `preserve-source`. *The test:* are the two halves interchangeable in sounding terms, or do they carry different shapes?

### similar-rhythm-different-function
Rising figures with similar rhythm but different function: one gathers into the quarter note, the longer phrase is kept apart. *Outcome:* the rhythmic similarity is a trap; the function differs, so nothing is supplied: **Intentional difference**, disposition `preserve-source`. *The opposite:* when the family shows the same function everywhere and only the seed lacks the slur, it is **Omission** and `supply-from-parallel` (as in `shared-gesture-parallel-part`). *The test:* function: ask what the figure does in the phrase, not what it looks like.

### fz-normalised-as-sf
A printed *fz* is rendered as *sf*. *Outcome:* the print's *fz* is unambiguous (**Intentional difference**: the mark is the source's own), and the edition normalises to the house's mark inventory, disposition `normalise-notation`. *The opposite:* when later sources consistently distinguish *fz* from *sf* and the work carries both with different meanings, normalising is a loss: `preserve-source`, and *fz* stands. *The test:* does the work carry both marks with distinct use?

### coordinated-release
A coordinated release: when the parts release together, it is written out. *Outcome:* one part's missing release was **Omission** against the coordinated gesture; disposition `coordinate-release`. *The opposite:* when the uncoordinated release is the source's own state (layers written separately, the difference recurring and pattern-bearing), it is **Intentional difference** and `preserve-source`. *The test:* does the family release together everywhere except the seed, or is the unevenness the rule?

## Outside the casebook

Ensemble semantics (dynamics differing per layer such as *f* against *ff*, entry dynamics for exposed solos, *a due*/*div.*/*Solo*/*Soli*, cues, brass alternatives, G.P. labels) belong to ensemble editing: the casebook invents no cases for them. Other editorial questions (viola slurs, a *forte* on beat two, uneven diminuendo ends, re-articulation before legato, optics, snapshots, prefaces) are carried by their own requirements, not by this catalogue.

## Review checklist

Before the casebook is handed over, and before a case is cited in a record:

1. Does every case carry its opposite outcome *in the text*, with when it applies?
2. Can any case be read as a norm? (If so: drop it or rewrite it, per the escalation rule above.)
3. Are the case names stable since the last reading (skills and records cite them)?
4. Does the case lead back to the hypothesis checklist (counter-evidence test, family rule) and to a disposition from the edition's disposition vocabulary, not to an opinion of its own?
5. Is every claim about a case read from evidence (family investigation done), not merely read from similarity?
6. Do the ensemble rows stay outside, with no invented cases?
